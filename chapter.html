Blood Relatives

An Inspector Griffin Mystery • Chapter One

Chapter One

Waterloo Station in London is a major train terminus handling nearly 60 million passengers a year. Adam Griffin was convinced they were all there today and all of them had been told to get in his way. He wasn't rushing but it was still frustrating.

As he left the station he was relieved to see there were a lot fewer people. At last, some breathing space. He started to set some music on his phone for the twenty minute walk home and put his ear buds in. The music was too loud so he looked down to adjust the volume.

In his peripheral vision he caught a flash of white and looked up sharply as a white van mounted the kerb just a few feet away. The road is supposed to be used for cabs and deliveries so normally the traffic is slow moving but this van was moving fast, and accelerating.

Almost transfixed, Adam started to move back when he felt a huge tug on his backpack, slamming him backwards until he fell in a tangled mess on top of his rescuer. The last thing Adam saw before he hit the ground was his phone flying into the air.

Oh shit, he thought, please don't break.

From the corner of his eye, a hand flew up and snatched the phone from the air, holding it aloft like Excalibur rising from the lake. Safe.

Suddenly there were lots of passers-by offering help and opinions – mostly opinions.

"It was a kid in a hoodie," someone said.

"Wearing a surgical mask," another voice joined in.

"I thought it was a girl driving," a third voice.

"Probably on the bloody phone." Everyone had an opinion.

Adam wasn't the centre of attention though. Another man had been about to rack up a Santander public bike across the road after he'd hired it. The van had caught the back wheel of the bike and essentially destroyed it. Snatched it from his hands. There was much discussion about whether he'd be liable and whether it was still on hire to him.

A woman who was about to step off the pavement and jog to work had been clipped on the arm by the door mirror. She was assuring everyone that she was a nurse working locally in Accident and Emergency and would get it checked at work.

Mostly people just kept walking past. Some people tutted as they had to step around the two bodies on the ground.

Adam took a deep breath and caught a scent in the air. Close by.

Chanel Pour Monsieur, he thought to himself. Classic fragrance. Stylish.

He rolled over, off the other body, and came up on to his hands and knees.

"Thank you," he said to his saviour, "that was stupidly close."

Coming up into a sitting position Finn Williams grinned back at him. "I thought that van driver had it in for you. I hope he had as much of a scare as me."

"I know! I think I'm jinxed. I fell under a tube train last month. Now this."

Adam tilted his head to one side and looked again. "I know you. We were at school together."

"You're right, but maybe we should get out of the way of all these people."

The both stood, pulling each other up and moved towards the building to recover.

"Finn, Finn Williams," said Finn, holding out his hand.

"Adam Griffin – we were in the same year but… didn't you disappear from school mysteriously."

"Long story but yes, it was just after you and I had a bit of a moment at a party." Finn wiggled his eyebrows and smiled.

"I remember it well," Adam laughed. "A pile of coats in a darkened bedroom I think."

"It was the week after my sixteenth birthday but someone else was having a house party. I seem to remember you said what we did was your favourite number."

"It still is." Adam beamed.

He gave Finn the once over, fairly subtly. Finn was about the same height as him, around 6 foot and looked to be in good shape. In very good shape in fact. They were both twenty one, both with dark hair but Adam had soft brown eyes compared to Finn's blue eyes.

Both liked what they saw.

"Listen, I'd really like to go for a coffee if you're free." said Adam.

Finn hesitated and grimaced. "I've got a circuit training class I really shouldn't miss."

Adam spoke before he thought. "Do you always wear Chanel cologne to the gym?" he smiled.

"What? Oh… well spotted… or sniffed maybe."

"I'm sorry," said Adam sheepishly. "I have this weird sense of smell. My Grandmother used to say I had a nose like a dog, which now I think about doesn't sound like a compliment."

They smiled at each other, both a little unsure.

"Don't worry," Finn replied. "At worst you could get a job at an airport. It's a classic though and I used to steal it from my dad but now it's my daily choice."

"I remember," said Adam, "it's my second best memory of that party."

Adam blushed a little, which Finn thought was attractive.

"But… if you're free tomorrow…"

"Deal!" said Adam. "I'm free all day tomorrow. No lectures. No commitments."

"How about 2pm ish."

"I know a fantastic little place that would be ideal. It's like a greasy spoon cafe but with the best coffee you've ever tasted."

"OK, let's swap numbers and you can message me the details."

They swapped phones and each entered their contact details. As he gave it back Adam held the phone for a second longer.

"Tomorrow you can tell me the long story of your mysterious disappearance. I missed you."

The boys locked eyes. They exchanged an unspoken agreement.

As they walked off in opposite directions they both had a small smile playing across their lips.

Finn got home from circuit training feeling tired but energised. He loved the communal feel of the class. A little competition from others and a little banter. He liked that each person could work to their limit without any pressure. As they went round the stations in the room each person worked on their own but with the comfort of seeing others suffering. It made him smile.

As he let himself in, his dad was sitting at the dining table looking at his laptop. He kissed his dad on the top of his head and felt an arm slide round his waist.

Marc Williams was in his late 40's and his once brown hair had gone steel grey, if you were optimistic but closer to white in reality. Finn hoped he had inherited those genes because he thought his dad looked pretty good. He was fit and in good shape – the whole family would run frequent half-marathons and regularly took part in park-runs.

"Did you have a good day?" he asked his dad.

"Not bad. No bad guys locked up but some progress made."

Detective Inspector Marcus Williams ran a unit for the Metropolitan Police investigating cyber crime and he loved his job.

"How about you?" he asked his son.

"Oh you know. Normal stuff. Rescued a cute guy from the path of a speeding van. He fell on top of me. There was a bit of a spark. Went to circuit training." Finn couldn't keep a straight face as he finished.

"A spark is it?" Finn had no reason to hide anything from his dad. "Well I hope this boy's intentions are honourable or I'll need my shotgun."

"We'll see…" said Finn as he pulled away from his dad. "I could do with some dishonourable intentions to be honest."

Walking upstairs to his room, Finn started to pull off his sweaty shirt. As he shut the door he stripped off completely and looked at himself in the full length mirror.

Not bad he thought. Good muscle definition, nice arms and a slightly defined six pack that didn't look AI enhanced. He thought about Adam with his cropped short dark hair and his eyes like velvety chocolate. He wondered what Adam would look like naked. Certainly different to when they had their last encounter. He'd filled out, looked more muscular. He looked like he took care of himself. He looked…

Oh-oh thought Finn, I need to stop thinking about Adam or I'll need to take care of myself too. He crossed the hallway into the bathroom. He didn't need to cover up because only his dad was home and anyway… nobody would mind.

As the shower warmed up Finn reflected on life in general. He knew he was lucky to be living in a stable home, in a really great location. He could walk to the gym near Waterloo Station in ten minutes. He could also walk to the Tate Modern art gallery, the theatres on the South Bank, Borough Market and just a bit further across the river was Soho, Covent Garden and all the attractions London had to offer.

He was benefiting from a police officer and a paramedic living together and qualifying as essential workers twice over. Originally they had rented their house, when the area was deeply unfashionable. Ultra-convenient but anywhere south of the river was considered undesirable.

Finn remembered, as a kid, the street had a lot of trouble and some unpleasant residents. There were several kids Finn was instructed to stay away from. Now, there was a TV director living next door. Three doors down was a Member of Parliament and there were at least three well known actors.

He was towelling himself off when he recalled Adam's parting comment about his mysterious disappearance.

Should I tell all? thought Finn. I don't want to scare him off. On the other hand, he wanted to be honest.

It had been a week and one day after that party, two weeks after his sixteenth birthday, when there had been a ferocious banging on his front door at 6am. Finn and his dad were alone in the house because paramedic shifts included nights.

Four large men in suits identified themselves to Marc and came in. Marc told them he was a police officer but they already knew that. After a brief conversation with his dad the pair of them were put in the back of a black Range Rover and driven off. The other two stayed at the house.

As the car crossed Westminster Bridge, it turned left just after Big Ben. Marc knew where they were heading and, unknown to him at the time, so did Finn.

They drove along Millbank and turned twice into a parallel road behind an impressive looking office block. They pulled up next to a single door with a small simple sign alongside saying Thames House.

Marc and Finn both knew where they were. They were at MI5, the home of British domestic intelligence.

They were led inside and the two burly men were replaced by a lovely looking young woman in her late 20's who led them to a meeting room. Marc was relieved it didn't look like an interrogation room but said nothing.

Within one minute a tall, slim man appeared through the door. He was well dressed, but dishevelled, like he'd been up all night. He was in his early 50's and didn't look friendly.

"Finn Williams…" he consulted the file in front of him. "We have evidence that you have gained unauthorised access to computers systems contrary to the Computer Misuse Act 1990 and by the unauthorised modification of computer material, under section three of that law you may be liable for an unlimited fine and up to ten years imprisonment."

Marc was shocked but kept a calm poker face. He'd been involved in many interviews like this and was also aware that Finn was a computing genius.

"What is this evidence against my son?" he asked.

"Well for starters, we have a text file containing the words 'Finn was here'."

Marc suppressed a nervous smile. "And what computer systems are we talking about?"

"Well, MI5 obviously," the man said, gesturing around. "And we are investigating further breaches. Further charges could follow you know."

"What other systems…" Marc started.

"We can hold you for up to 14 days without charge under the Terrorism Act you know."

"I am well aware of the law, thanks."

"MI6…" Finn said confidently. Both men turned to look at him. "And the government communications centre at GCHQ. Border Force, the Foreign Office, more or less all the major utility companies, all the big banks and 27 giant corporations. Check it out… 'Finn was here' is all over the place."

"This is a very serious matter young man…" the unidentified man started to say as the door opened.

"Thank you Robert, I'll take over now." A newcomer entered the room carrying a single, thin, file. He was polished and immaculately dressed. Very shiny shoes that Marc recognised as hand-made.

He sat, exuding the sort of confidence that came with a very expensive education and a rarefied, cultured existence. This was a chief, not an indian.

He didn't introduce himself. He was the sort of man who you just knew was important.

"Well Finn," he smiled at Finn, "I have three choices open to me. Perhaps you, and your father, can help me make the right decision."

Marc narrowed his eyes. He felt this man had already made his mind up but he had no choice but to let him play out his game.

"I could have you prosecuted, as my colleague said, under the Computer Misuse Act with a maximum sentence of 10 years."

He held out one hand as if weighing that option, then extending another hand to balance.

"Or… I could have you both taken to the basement and shot in the head."

He gave a thin smile, clearly enjoying the theatricality of his little show.

"Or…?" said Marc.

"Maybe there is no other option," the polished gentleman replied.

Marc took a deep breath to reply but Finn spoke. Quietly but with confidence.

"Computers are pure logic," he said. "If you do x and y the answer is always z. If the answer doesn't compute, it means you've made a mistake somewhere."

Finn wasn't feeling at all confident but he knew he couldn't show that. Suddenly, spiders popped into his head. They're more scared of you than you are of them, he thought.

He smiled slowly, unnervingly, across the table.

"Option one won't fly because you'd need to admit in open court that a sixteen year old kid accessed one of the most secure systems in the country. If a kid could do it, what could Russia do? Or China?"

The man brushed non-existent fluff from his immaculately pressed trousers.

"Option two sounds attractive but it doesn't answer any of your questions. The how, when and why. And… compellingly, what else."

Finn smiled and, surprisingly, the man smiled back.

"So, logic dictates that your file contains a copy of the Official Secrets Act for me to sign and a job offer."

The man looked at Marc across the table. "Don't you just hate young people?" and he pushed the file across the table.

And that's how it started. A 16 year old schoolboy recruited by the intelligence services as a good way of shutting him up and tapping into his astonishing grasp of cyber security.
