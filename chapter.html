Blood Relatives

An Inspector Griffin Mystery • Chapter One

Chapter One

Waterloo Station in London is a major train terminus handling nearly 60 million passengers a year. Adam Griffin was convinced they were all there today and all of them had been told to get in his way. He wasn't rushing but it was still frustrating.

As he left the station he was relieved to see there were a lot fewer people. At last, some breathing space. He started to set some music on his phone for the twenty minute walk home and put his ear buds in. The music was too loud so he looked down to adjust the volume.

In his peripheral vision he caught a flash of white and looked up sharply as a white van mounted the kerb just a few feet away. The road is supposed to be used for cabs and deliveries so normally the traffic is slow moving but this van was moving fast, and accelerating.

Almost transfixed, Adam started to move back when he felt a huge tug on his backpack, slamming him backwards until he fell in a tangled mess on top of his rescuer. The last thing Adam saw before he hit the ground was his phone flying into the air.

Oh shit, he thought, please don't break.

From the corner of his eye, a hand flew up and snatched the phone from the air, holding it aloft like Excalibur rising from the lake. Safe.

Suddenly there were lots of passers-by offering help and opinions – mostly opinions.

"It was a kid in a hoodie," someone said.

"Wearing a surgical mask," another voice joined in.

"I thought it was a girl driving," a third voice.

"Probably on the bloody phone." Everyone had an opinion.

Adam wasn't the centre of attention though. Another man had been about to rack up a Santander public bike across the road after he'd hired it. The van had caught the back wheel of the bike and essentially destroyed it. Snatched it from his hands. There was much discussion about whether he'd be liable and whether it was still on hire to him.

A woman who was about to step off the pavement and jog to work had been clipped on the arm by the door mirror. She was assuring everyone that she was a nurse working locally in Accident and Emergency and would get it checked at work.

Mostly people just kept walking past. Some people tutted as they had to step around the two bodies on the ground.
